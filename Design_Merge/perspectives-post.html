文章標題

一段簡短的文章介紹文字，說明這篇觀點在談什麼。

Dutch contemporary artist Bobbi Essers and Brussels-based artist Robin Wen star in a two-person exhibition at MuseumMORE in Gorssel. The curatorial premise sets two visions of a generation’s nightlife side by side: Essers in oil on canvas, working from analogue photographs of her friends; Wen in hyperrealistic blue ballpoint pen drawings rooted in Free Party and rave culture, where his subjects appear in empty party sites at dawn or as faceless dancing figures.

《最遠的最短距離》從一個簡單的問題出發：當我們搬進一個新的住處，要花多久，才會真正覺得那裡是「家」？林珈伃以三年間在台中、台北與東京之間搬遷的經驗為起點，收集了每一個住處留下的影像、物件與筆記。

展場中散落的小幅照片，記錄的不是房間本身，而是那些還沒被歸位的時刻：剛拆封的紙箱、尚未掛上的窗簾、牆上留下的前任住戶的釘孔。這些畫面被放置在透明壓克力之間，像是被暫時保存、卻還沒有決定去向的記憶。In the work, distance is not measured in kilometres, but in the time it takes for a place to remember you back.

展覽同時呈現藝術家與觀眾的對話紀錄。開展期間，林珈伃邀請觀眾寫下自己「最遠的最短距離」——也許是一通沒有打出去的電話，也許是住在隔壁卻從未說過話的鄰居。這些回應將持續被加入展場，讓作品在展期中慢慢長大。

The museum frames Essers’ contribution around queer positivity, deliberately ambiguous gender and identity, and the rituals of friendship and the after-hours. Works in the show include In a New York State of Mind (2025), Love Ridden (I’ve Looked at You) (2025), and Nothing Makes Me Feel As Good (2025). MuseumMORE had previously included Essers in the group shows Licked by the Waves (2024) and Reality Check (2025). Wen graduated from La Cambre in Brussels in 2018 and won the Woluwe-Saint-Pierre Arts Prize; this is his first appearance in a Dutch museum.
